University oil spill research

Several major U.S. universities do active oil spill research and response work — including two right here in California.

USF College of Marine Science

Oil Spill Research Program

Home of C-IMAGE, a ten-year, $36.6 million research program studying the impact of oil spills on the Gulf of Mexico, alongside ongoing satellite monitoring and pollution studies.

 USF College of Marine Science

Recent Study: Oil Spills Spiked in the Persian Gulf

A 2026 study using satellite data found spill coverage roughly quadrupled amid regional conflict — a current example of USF's active spill-monitoring work.

 USF College of Marine Science

Oil Spill Information Archive

USF's long-running archive of Deepwater Horizon-era research, news coverage, and findings from over a decade of Gulf oil spill science.

 UC San Diego — Scripps Institution of Oceanography

Real-Time Ocean Monitoring During Spill Response

During the 2021 Huntington Beach spill, Scripps deployed sensor-equipped ocean robots and radar-based current tracking to feed real-time data to spill responders — based right here in Southern California.

 UC Davis — Oiled Wildlife Care Network

Oiled Wildlife Care Network (OWCN)

A UC Davis-managed statewide network that rescues and rehabilitates oil-affected wildlife across California, funding millions in research on treatment and long-term recovery since 1994.

Real cleanup technology breakthroughs

Beyond traditional booms and skimmers, researchers have developed genuinely new materials and methods for pulling oil out of water.

MIT

Magnetic Nanoparticle Oil Separation

MIT researchers developed a patented method that mixes water-repellent magnetic nanoparticles into spilled oil, then uses magnets to lift the oil cleanly out of the water — with the oil itself recoverable afterward.

 Northwestern University

Reusable "Smart Sponge"

A porous sponge coated in an oil-attracting, water-repelling nanocomposite that absorbs over 30 times its own weight in oil and can be squeezed out and reused dozens of times.

 World Economic Forum

A global coalition tracking pledges from governments, companies, and organizations toward growing and restoring a trillion trees by 2030.
